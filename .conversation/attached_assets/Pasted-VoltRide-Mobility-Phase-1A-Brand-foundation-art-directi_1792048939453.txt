VoltRide Mobility — Phase 1A
Brand foundation, art direction and website-wide design system
Master website specification · Design phase 1 of 3 · Sub-phase 1A
Phase 1A establishes the visual and experiential foundation for the entire VoltRide Mobility website. Before designing individual pages, we define how the brand looks, feels, communicates, presents its coaches and expresses the relationship between the commercial entities.
The goal is a website that feels like an established electric mobility and transport infrastructure business—not a generic EV startup, a bus dealership template or a futuristic technology demo.




















9








1. Design objective
VoltRide Mobility must communicate three things immediately:
1. The asset: electric intercity coaches that transport operators can explore leasing or purchasing.
2. The system: a connected ecosystem of coach supply, battery solutions, charging infrastructure and operational coordination.
3. The commercial pathway: a clear process for evaluating the right arrangement for each fleet.
The website should be visually confident and technically credible, with the coach itself as the primary visual subject. Infrastructure, people, data and technology should support that story rather than compete with it.
2. Brand positioning and hierarchy
PRIMARY BRAND
VOLTRIDE MOBILITY
Electric mobility. Connected by design.

Coach ownership and leasing
VoltRide owns the coaches and enters into coach-leasing contracts. It also presents outright-purchase options where available, with the legal ownership transfer specified in each sale contract.


Integrated mobility services
Customers can explore battery leasing, charging infrastructure and fleet operations through the wider ecosystem.


Coordinated delivery
Autovance coordinates operations and the customer journey, while each specialist business remains responsible for its contracted services.

The website must not make every entity appear to be a separate, competing customer brand. VoltRide leads the experience; the other entities appear as clearly defined specialist partners within the wider system.

Colour application
- Graphite: navigation, headlines, footer, key buttons and major brand statements.
- Steel blue: secondary actions, technical diagrams, section labels and supporting emphasis.
- Technical white: the principal canvas for content, specifications and product presentation.
- Silver grey: dividers, cards, tables, borders and subtle interface backgrounds.
- Deep slate: occasional dark panels for fleet technology, charging and operational storytelling.
Avoid using green as the dominant colour simply because the product is electric. VoltRide should own its distinctive graphite-and-steel visual territory.
Typography
Use a contemporary geometric sans-serif with strong legibility and controlled spacing.

4. Photography and image art direction
Photography will be one of the strongest determinants of perceived quality.









A. The coach
Use full-size, realistic electric intercity coaches with convincing proportions, glazing, wheels and bodywork. Hero images should show the vehicle clearly, preferably in three-quarter view, with room for text placement.












B. The operating environment
Show real-world intercity travel, terminals, highways and passenger transport settings. For a Kenyan launch, regional context should feel authentic rather than relying exclusively on European landscapes.












C. The infrastructure
Photograph charging equipment, depot layouts, power systems and service environments as tangible engineering assets—not abstract neon circuitry.












D. The passenger experience
Show spacious interiors, seating, luggage provision and passenger comfort where actual vehicle specifications support those representations.



OEM imagery rules: King Long, Yutong and Zhongtong vehicles must be shown with their correct manufacturer identities and verified model details. Do not use a generic generated electric coach as though it were a specific OEM model. Label conceptual illustrations where necessary, and use authorised OEM photography or verified assets for actual product listings.
5. Layout and component system
The site should use a consistent twelve-column desktop grid, a fluid tablet layout and a simplified mobile composition.
Core layout principles
Editorial composition
Wide photographic hero sections, strong alignment and short text blocks. Avoid filling every available space with cards.


Reusable components
Buttons, product cards, specification tables, OEM cards, service panels, form controls, navigation and footers must share consistent spacing and behaviour.


Mobile-first conversion
The fleet assessment call to action must remain easy to locate. Forms should use stacked fields, concise labels and touch-friendly controls.


Accessibility
Use semantic headings, visible keyboard focus, readable contrast, descriptive image alternatives and reduced-motion support.



Buttons
- Primary: graphite background, white text, restrained radius.
- Secondary: white or transparent background, graphite border and text.
- Text links: understated, with a clear hover and focus state.
- Avoid pill-shaped buttons everywhere, oversized gradients or multiple competing primary actions.
Suggested action hierarchy:
1. Request a fleet assessment
2. Explore coach options
3. Compare commercial models
4. Explore charging solutions
6. Motion and interaction principles
Motion should communicate precision, not spectacle.
- Use subtle transitions for navigation, buttons and expandable information.
- Allow the three-line aerodynamic motif to inspire occasional directional movement.
- Keep hero text readable and stable; avoid aggressive typewriter effects or moving text that harms accessibility.
- Use gentle image transitions and restrained scroll reveals.
- Keep technical diagrams static or minimally animated by default.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and ensure the site remains fully usable without animation.
